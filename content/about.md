I write the firmware that lives inside professional audio amplifiers: the code on the
microcontroller that drives the amp, moves audio samples around and processes the signal
in real time. When it works, nobody notices. That's the goal.

<p class="section-label">What I work on</p>

- **Microcontrollers:** STM32, NXP
- **RTOS:** FreeRTOS, Zephyr
- **Digital audio links:** I²S, TDM, A2B
- **Audio over IP:** AES67, Dante, networking on lwIP

<p class="section-label">Agent-assisted coding</p>

Embedded development is changing, and AI is a big part of that change. I work with AI
agents as part of my daily firmware workflow, an approach I like to call
**agent-assisted coding**. I'm interested in how it can push embedded development forward,
on real hardware with real constraints: tight memory, hard timing, no room for guesswork.
